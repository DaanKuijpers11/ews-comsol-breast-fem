\section{Conclusion}

This project developed a reproducible COMSOL finite-element workflow for an anatomically richer breast model aimed at deformation-based Early Warning Scan model development. The model route included a curved posterior chestwall support, a realistic fibroglandular tissue distribution, material and skin sensitivity routes, simplified Cooper-like support definitions, dynamic loading variants and analytic tumor-overlay placement.

The strongest quantitative sensitivity was observed for the material and skin-parameter variants. The volumetric skin representation and associated stiffness choices had a large influence on peak displacement and local von Mises stress concentrations. The simplified Cooper-like support route produced only small global displacement changes in the available scout results, and should therefore be interpreted as a support-sensitivity implementation rather than as a validated anatomical ligament model.

The tumor-overlay route was successfully implemented at build level and visualised for different tumor sizes and locations. These results show that the analytic spherical tumor mask can be placed inside the anatomical breast model, but they do not yet provide quantitative evidence for tumor detectability. Complete tumor-sensitivity studies require matched no-tumor controls, fully solved dynamic cases, surface-based post-processing and validation against experimental or EWS-like surface-motion data. Overall, the project provides a traceable COMSOL FEM platform for future EWS-oriented breast deformation studies, while showing that stronger computational resources are needed for systematic large-scale simulation and validation.
